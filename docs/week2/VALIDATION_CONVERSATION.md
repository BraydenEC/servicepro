# 🗣 Human Validation Conversation — template

**Required by the Week 2 assignment: 1 real human validation conversation.**

This is the only deliverable in the entire project that a coding agent cannot produce. It cannot
be simulated, drafted, or reconstructed from research. Fill it in after an actual conversation
with an actual person.

---

## Who to talk to

One freelancer, contractor, designer, developer, or consultant. **Ideally in Mexico**, because the
CFDI finding is the sharpest claim in the research and the only person who can confirm or destroy
it is someone who actually invoices there.

If nobody in Mexico is reachable, a freelancer anywhere still tests the *general* premise — that
project, time, and invoice data being split across tools costs real money and attention. Note
which version you tested.

**Where to find one:** classmates who freelance, a designer or developer you have hired,
freelancer groups on WhatsApp or Facebook, a contador who works with freelance clients (they see
this problem across dozens of people at once), or a family friend who invoices independently.

---

## The rule that makes this worth doing

**Ask open questions and do not defend the product.** A conversation that confirms everything is
usually a conversation with leading questions. You are trying to find out whether you are wrong
while it is still cheap.

Do not say *"would you use a tool that combines project tracking and CFDI?"* — almost everyone
says yes to a hypothetical. Ask what they do now, and listen for friction they have already paid
for with their own time.

---

## Questions

1. How do you currently keep track of what clients owe you?
2. Walk me through what happens between finishing a piece of work and getting paid.
3. What do you use to invoice? How long does it take you?
4. **(Mexico)** How do you handle facturación / CFDI? Is that the same tool, or a different one?
5. **(Mexico)** Who actually issues the CFDI — you, your contador, or a platform?
6. What is the most annoying part of the admin side of freelancing?
7. Have you ever tried a tool for this? What happened — did you stick with it?
8. What would have to be true for you to switch to something new?

### ⭐ What contradicted your assumptions

*The most valuable section in this document, and the one most likely to be left blank.*

If nothing here surprised you, that is itself a finding worth stating — but check first whether
the questions were leading. Things worth listening for:

- They do **not** find this painful, and have a workaround that already suits them
- Their contador handles all of it, so the CFDI gap is not theirs to feel
- They invoice so rarely that tooling is irrelevant
- The real pain is somewhere else entirely — chasing late payments, quoting, finding clients

---

### What this changes

*Does the research still hold? Does the product thesis need to move? Be specific.*

## How this feeds the submission

Whatever comes out of this belongs in the Human Decision Note. **A conversation that partly
contradicted you is stronger evidence of real research than one that agreed with everything** —
it demonstrates you asked questions capable of returning an unwelcome answer.
